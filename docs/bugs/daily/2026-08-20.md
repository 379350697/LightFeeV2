### Historical Versus New

The unpaid/provisional evidence and historical residual records are old close-accounting data debt. Their source family is CL-114: a non-terminal partial could be treated as terminal, exact reconciliation could reject its `average_price`, and an uncertain close could lose its CID. The deployed baseline prevents new instances of those specific input-loss paths, but it does not manufacture the exact historical fills needed to settle old owners.

The newly confirmed issue is downstream of that debt. When normal close reconciliation or passive-close processing removes a terminal accounting owner, `LiveRuntime` can retain its already-built recovery ledger. The V1 lifecycle core then still sees recovery work that no longer exists in `EngineState`, so a stale owner can keep a lifecycle/risk-only conclusion alive until another full ledger rebuild. This is a cleanup/invalidation gap, not evidence that the current paired COTI position is residual exposure.

The diagnostic's historical `residual_events_present` signal is intentionally not waived while the account is non-flat. It is a conservative deployment gate, not proof by itself that a residual-repair task is currently active.

### Authoritative Contract

V1 semantics require owner state to be derived from durable state plus trusted exchange truth. Once a close reconciliation has reached its terminal path, the removed owner must not remain in the recovery ledger or V1 lifecycle closure. Conversely, missing identity, partial execution evidence, non-flat exchange truth, or a retained reconciliation must continue to block release.

### Root Cause and Candidate Shared-Boundary Repair

When `CloseRuntime` removes a pending close reconciliation, its caller did not refresh the recovery ledger that was built before processing. The passive-close driver had the same omission, including two sibling outcomes that do not necessarily remove a reconciliation: a fully accounted passive close disappears directly, or a passive owner hands off to a newly registered accounting owner. The repair snapshots the two close-owner collections represented by the ledger (pending reconciliations and pending passive closes) around both production callers, and rebuilds the ledger only when either owner count changes. It therefore covers removal, direct completion, and passive-to-accounting handoff without changing close submission, accounting evidence, or the fail-closed retain path.

### Same-Family Audit

- The two production callers are now both covered: pending-entry housekeeping invokes `LiveRuntime._process_pending_close_reconciliations`, and the normal tick invokes `LiveRuntime._maybe_tick_passive_close`. There is no third direct V2 caller for either close processor.
- Startup replay reconstructs `EngineState` before its first recovery ledger is built, so it cannot retain this in-memory ledger cache across that reconstruction boundary.
- Pending-entry terminalization already uses its own symbol-truth ledger refresh; it is not a duplicate of this close-owner cache gap.
- Residual-repair completion independently rebuilds `RecoveryLedger` from current local state before it updates the recovery decision. Its regression includes a deliberately stale cached-ledger veto and proves the entry gate is released only after the residual task is gone. No additional residual change is justified by the current evidence.
- Owner status changes that leave both collections at the same cardinality intentionally do not refresh: the owner itself remains unresolved. The retained/evidence-gap paths stay fail-closed.

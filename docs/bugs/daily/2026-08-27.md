# Bug Ledger Daily - 2026-08-27

## CL-126: Passive-close ACK identity loss and stranded evidence debt

Status: `local-green`, not production-closed. Fix commit: `ac9d536`.

### Symptom and evidence

Production retained three final `known_close_fill_temporarily_unavailable`
debts (two COTI, one BTR) after both exchange legs and all open orders were
confirmed flat. They are accounting/audit debts, not residual positions. Their
per-leg records retained client IDs, while some acknowledged exchange order IDs
survived only in unrelated global payloads and could not be safely attributed
to a long or short leg. The historical matcher existed, but this debt reason
was restricted to exact-ID retries and never reached that matcher when the
stored ID/CID no longer returned a complete execution.

### Authoritative contract

V1 close recovery keeps every submitted close identity through the passive
close owner until the final reconciliation has exact execution and fee evidence.
An accepted ACK is identity evidence, not a fill. A close may be physically
flat before execution history becomes queryable, but it must not then guess
which leg an order belongs to or turn ambiguous history into PnL.

### Root cause

V2 persisted the pre-submit CID in `PersistedCloseExecutionLeg`, but successful
maker/amend/hedge ACKs updated transient phase fields or ordinary journal
records only. A crash or live-flat handoff after ACK could therefore retain a
CID-only leg even when the exchange order ID was known. Cancel/replace also
risked overwriting the active ID instead of preserving every acknowledged
identity. The parallel aggressive-close path also converted a zero-fill ACK
into `order_id=""`; if a later retry filled, it discarded the earlier uncertain
submission entirely. Separately, `known_close_fill_temporarily_unavailable`
did exact retries only, unlike the existing strict historical recovery path
used for missing identities.

### Repair and rejected directions

The passive-close owner now stores `order_id` beside `client_order_id`; each
successful ACK is written as a critical full-owner journal event and replayed
into the same per-leg record. Distinct acknowledged IDs are preserved across
amend/cancel-replace. The wiring covers maker submit, maker amend, hedge,
duplicate-CID retry, one-sided flatten, imbalanced-excess flatten, and typed
accepted-order truth gaps.

The aggressive executor now carries every uncertain submission through retry
and reconciliation as its `(exchange order ID, CID)` pair. A later fill cannot
erase an earlier accepted or zero-fill ACK. CID-only retries retain the former
idempotency behavior; a distinct exchange order ID with the same CID is added
as a second exact lookup key, not collapsed into the known fill.

The same invariant now covers V1-style emergency compensation. Its typed
outcome carries confirmed partial fills, exact unconfirmed ACK identities, and
the independent exchange-flat result. A flat position probe may end exposure
repair, but never deletes an accepted compensation order ID. A partial
compensation fill followed by a retry records both fills; a zero-fill
compensation ACK followed by a flat probe becomes a precise reconciliation
placeholder rather than an invisible accounting gap.

For final evidence debt, known IDs are queried first. Only an incomplete exact
result may enter the existing bounded historical path, which still requires
both venues flat, no open orders, an expected close quantity, exactly one
symbol/side/position-side/time-window candidate, and the adapter's exact
execution-and-fee recheck. Missing quantity snapshots retain V1's exact-ID
retry behavior; they cannot enter history discovery. Ambiguous, partial, or
otherwise deterministically incomplete strict history is retained as
`irrecoverable_audit_debt`: it stays visible for a manual exact import but is
never auto-polled again, billed, or used to reopen a position. Exchange-truth
and transport failures remain retryable because no completed strict query has
occurred.

Do not recover these debts by joining global order ID lists to legs by order,
time, or quantity; by treating CID-only evidence as a fill; by broadening the
history window; or by unbounded polling. Those methods recreate silent
misattribution rather than repairing evidence ownership.

### Counterexamples and verification

| Case | Required outcome |
|---|---|
| ACK after a snapshot, then restart | exact `order_id` remains on the original leg |
| Cancel/replace ACKs share a CID | old and replacement order identities both remain |
| Exact records arrive late | settle without history discovery |
| Exact recheck stays incomplete, one strict history candidate | settle only after exact historical execution/fee proof |
| Missing quantity snapshot with known IDs | exact retry remains allowed; history discovery is blocked |
| Completed ambiguous/no-candidate/incomplete strict history | retain `irrecoverable_audit_debt`, no automatic retry or bill |
| Non-flat/open-order truth or history transport failure | retain retryable debt with backoff; never bill |
| Aggressive zero-fill ACK with an order ID | final debt retains that exact ID and resolves by exact lookup |
| Aggressive retry fills after a zero-fill ACK | both order IDs remain; no terminal bill before both are reconciled |
| Compensation zero-fill ACK then exchange-flat probe | final debt retains the compensation order ID and resolves by exact lookup |
| Compensation partial fill then retry | both actual fills are retained in the final close accounting |

Close-path selection (`tests/test_passive_close.py`, automatic historical
evidence, ACK/truth-gap recovery, and state persistence) passed `349` tests;
the full validation profile passed all 10 steps. This includes the terminal
strict-history, retryable transport, and post-crash replay counterexamples.

### Production acceptance

After deployment, verify a new passive close emits
`exit.close_order_identity_acknowledged` before final live-flat handoff and its
pending reconciliation contains both per-leg ID and CID. Observe that delayed
execution history either settles automatically under the strict contract or
remains a visible debt. Re-run the existing three debts once through this
bounded path; mark any that lack a unique exact reconstruction as irrecoverable
audit debt, never inferred PnL. For aggressive closes, do not induce traffic:
only
observe that `order.uncertain` retains any returned `order_id` and a retry does
not produce `exit.closed` until the associated exact reconciliation completes.

## CL-127: Sidecar health false-green on stale source evidence

Status: `local-green`, not production-closed. Fix commit: `ac9d536`.

### Root cause and repair

The old health gate checked only the wrapper snapshot's publication time. A
sidecar that continued publishing last-good data therefore appeared fresh even
when an individual venue's quote/funding/liquidity source was stale or
degraded; the OKX incident was caught only incidentally by the socket alert.
The entry path already applies domain-specific source-age budgets, so the gate
now reads the live service's configured TOML and uses that same central policy.
It rejects missing/stale quote timestamps, missing/stale funding/market/
liquidity lifecycle source evidence, nonpositive coverage, and all explicit
degraded venue/domain/symbol markers.

No entry threshold, venue coverage rule, or sidecar publication behavior was
changed. The repair only makes deployment admission fail when the existing
entry evidence is already unfit.

### Counterexamples and verification

| Case | Required outcome |
|---|---|
| Fresh `published_at_ms`, degraded OKX lifecycle | critical health failure |
| Fresh wrapper, old quote source timestamp | critical health failure |
| Missing lifecycle row/source timestamp | critical health failure |
| Seven venues with fresh source rows and no degradation | health passes |
| Missing or unreadable live config | deployment admission fails, never falls back silently |

The joint venue/health/diagnosis selection passed `223` tests and the full
validation profile passed all 10 steps. Production acceptance must prove a
normal fresh seven-venue snapshot and verify that an artificially fresh wrapper
time cannot mask any source-age failure.

## CL-128: Diagnosis omitted rotated journals and drifted the ledger

Status: `local-green`, not production-closed. Fix commit: `ac9d536`.

### Root cause and repair

`diagnose_live.py` only matched names ending in `.jsonl`, silently omitting the
active journal rotations such as `live-events.jsonl.1`. Its tail reader also
fell back to the beginning of a file when a high-confidence deployment window
had fewer than its record cap, so a large active journal could omit the newest
rejection. The diagnosis could consequently disagree with production reality
and the bug ledger's recorded deployment state.

The single journal-read boundary now discovers canonical journal rotations,
reads complete JSONL records backwards in bounded chunks, merges in physical
newest-first rotation order under one global record cap, de-duplicates only
same `(run_id, seq)` records, then time-sorts the retained evidence. Output
records which files were read or intentionally excluded and whether the global
cap was reached; it does not present an incomplete cap-limited scan as a full
history.

### Counterexamples and verification

| Case | Required outcome |
|---|---|
| Important reject exists only in `.jsonl.1` | diagnosed exactly once |
| Same `(run_id, seq)` appears in active and rotated file | diagnosed once, not suppressed by kind/time coincidence |
| 25k old records plus a recent tail reject in `since-deploy` window | tail reject remains visible |
| Event cap reached | scope reports the cap and skipped files |

`tests/test_diagnose_live.py` passed `117` tests; the full validation profile
passed all 10 steps. The production probe must record all discovered rotations
and its scope metadata before this entry can close.
